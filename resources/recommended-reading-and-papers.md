# Recommended System Design Literature & Canonical Papers 📚

Curated collection of seminal research papers, engineering blogs, and definitive books that form the foundation of modern distributed systems.

---

## 📖 Canonical Books

1. **Designing Data-Intensive Applications (DDIA)** by Martin Kleppmann
   - *The undisputed bible of modern distributed systems, storage engines, and consensus.*
2. **System Design Interview – An Insider's Guide (Vols 1 & 2)** by Alex Xu
   - *Clear visual guides for standard interview case studies.*
3. **Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)** by Betsy Beyer et al. (Google)
   - *Practical operations, SLOs, error budgets, and incident postmortems.*
4. **Building Microservices (2nd Edition)** by Sam Newman
   - *Decomposition strategies, bounded contexts, and service communication.*
5. **Database Internals** by Alex Petrov
   - *Deep dive into B-Trees, LSM-Trees, distributed storage, and consensus.*

---

## 📄 Seminal Research Papers

- **Google File System (GFS)** (Ghemawat et al., 2003) — Distributed storage across commodity hardware.
- **MapReduce: Simplified Data Processing on Large Clusters** (Dean & Ghemawat, 2004) — Parallel batch processing.
- **Bigtable: A Distributed Storage System for Structured Data** (Chang et al., 2006) — Wide-column storage.
- **Dynamo: Amazon’s Highly Available Key-value Store** (DeCandia et al., 2007) — Consistent hashing, hinted handoff, and quorums.
- **In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm (Raft)** (Ongaro & Ousterhout, 2014) — Understandable consensus.
- **Spanner: Google’s Globally-Distributed Database** (Corbett et al., 2012) — External consistency with TrueTime atomic clocks.
- **Kafka: a Distributed Messaging System for Log Processing** (Kreps et al., 2011) — Distributed append-only commit log.
